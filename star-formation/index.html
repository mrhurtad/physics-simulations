04 About the PhysicsMODEL & LIMITATIONS

Cold gas, competing forces

A molecular cloud is cold interstellar gas, mainly molecular hydrogen. Gravity pulls its material together. A pressure gradient pushes gas away from dense regions. Here, pressure is calculated from particle densities; it is not random motion added to the picture.

cₛ = √(kBT / μmH)   ·   P = ρcₛ²

The model holds temperature fixed, approximating efficient cooling. Warm unconfined clouds can expand rather than remain in equilibrium.

When can a cloud collapse?

The Jeans mass estimates when self-gravity overcomes thermal support. Gas with M > MJ is flagged as gravitationally unstable. This estimate assumes a uniform, nonrotating medium; it is a diagnostic, not a timer or a guarantee.

MJ = (π5/2 / 6) cₛ³ / (Geff3/2 √ρ)

Here M = 90% of remaining gas mass, and ρ is its mean density inside R₉₀. The sink is excluded from that gas diagnostic.

Rotation and a disk

The initial velocity is v = ω₀ × r. Pair forces conserve angular momentum. During contraction, material spins faster and high angular momentum gas cannot fall directly into the center. Gas collisions dissipate converging motion and can leave a flattened rotating structure.

L = Σ m(r × v)   ·   Rc ≈ j² / (GeffMcentral)

In a disk dominated by the central object, vφ ≈ √(GeffMcentral/r). Different orbital radii therefore have different angular speeds. The disk diagnostic selects flattened, bound, rotationally supported gas; no decorative disk is inserted.

A protostar is not a main-sequence star

A single central sink represents an unresolved collapsing core and subsequent protostar. It forms only when gas within 160 AU is dense, converging, gravitationally bound, Jeans unstable, and near the potential minimum. Bound, inward-moving gas can accrete if its angular momentum permits entry.

The luminous marker scales schematically with sink mass. It is not a temperature measurement, radiative-transfer calculation, or evidence of sustained hydrogen fusion. Real protostars shine largely through contraction and accretion.

What the model resolves

512 equal-mass gas elements evolve in three spatial dimensions using softened pair gravity and SPH-inspired isothermal pressure. The starting sphere has radius 5,000 AU and an inward-increasing density profile (ρ ∝ 1/r, regularized by finite sampling and smoothing). The same deterministic sample is reused for every run. Both camera views project the actual computed positions.

Artificial viscosity dissipates converging flow, with heat assumed radiated away. Gravity is softened over 60 AU. Adaptive smoothing has an 80 AU floor; structure below a few smoothing lengths is unresolved. Total gas plus sink mass, momentum, and angular momentum are accounted for.

What it simplifies

This is a coarse educational gas model, not a research hydrodynamics or MHD solver. It omits magnetic fields, radiation transport, first-core heating, feedback, fragmentation into multiple stars, detailed stellar evolution, and planet formation. Adaptive smoothing omits grad-h corrections. Numerical viscosity can overestimate disk transport; disk sizes and accretion histories are qualitative and resolution dependent.

Runs end at 250 kyr. Low neighbour counts in dispersed gas and disks with few elements are flagged. The model has no external confining pressure, and no particles are discarded when they leave the view.
